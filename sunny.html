Parents wait years. Nobody answers their questions.

In Ontario, many families wait a long time for a pediatric autism diagnosis. While they wait, they have real questions about funding, next steps and which program to apply for.

Clinic front desks answer the same questions every week. Often the questions come after hours, when nobody picks up. Families are left in silence, and clinics lose trust and leads.

The goal: give every family a clear next step, day or night, without adding work for the clinic.

One assistant, on the phone and on the website.

I designed and built everything myself: the research, the conversation design, the Ontario program knowledge, the phone agent, the website chat and the marketing website.

    Picks up 24/7 in warm, plain language.

  • Website chat

  • Text links

  • Booking

Three design decisions that matter

These are real conversations from Sunny. They show how I designed her to behave when the answer is not simple.

Ask first, then answer

A generic chatbot names a program right away. But the right program depends on one thing: does the child already have a diagnosis? Sunny asks that first, then gives a clear next step.

Is there any programs I should apply for autism?

Does he already have an autism diagnosis, or are you still working on getting one?

He's 3, not diagnosed yet.

Since he's not diagnosed yet, the best first step is a SmartStart Hub for early screening. Want me to text you the link?

Be honest, never leave a dead end

Sunny does not say what a parent wants to hear. If a program does not apply, she says so, then points to what does. This protects the family and the clinic's credibility.

My son was just diagnosed with ADHD. Does that qualify for OAP or Passport?

I want to be honest with you: ADHD on its own doesn't qualify for OAP or DSO/Passport. But SSAH and ACSD can. They look at documented functional limitations, not a specific diagnosis, so it's worth applying.

Safety comes first, always

Sunny never diagnoses and never gives medical advice. In an emergency she stops everything else and sends the parent to 911. She does not get pulled back into the topic until the parent is ready.

My child won't wake up!

Please call 911 right now. This needs emergency help immediately.

What about a diagnosis though?

911 first. Your child's safety comes before anything else. I'm here when you're ready.

Test notes: what broke, and what I changed.

No real family or clinic has used Sunny yet. These notes come from my own test calls and chats in Voiceflow's test tools, before any pilot.

For each test I read the transcript, found what failed, fixed it, and ran the test again. Here is what I found.

Conversation

  • What went wrong

    A caller said "I'm not sure why I'm calling." Sunny asked for a child's age, even though there might be no child.

    What I changed

    She now finds out who she is helping first. She asks for an age only when the call is about a child.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    She named a program that needs a diagnosis before asking if the child had one.

    What I changed

    I added a diagnosis check before she names a program. One live test still named OAP first, so I am watching this one.

    Still watching
  • What went wrong

    She gave a Ministry phone number that was not in her knowledge base. She had invented it.

    What I changed

    I removed it and added a rule: never make up phone numbers, addresses, prices or hours.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    She pushed a booking after every answer, like a salesperson.

    What I changed

    She reads the moment. She keeps first questions low pressure, and suggests booking only when the caller shows real interest.

    Fixed

Voice

  • What went wrong

    Speech recognition heard "seven" as "67" for a child's age.

    What I changed

    Sunny now repeats the age back to confirm before she goes on.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    Speech recognition heard "hot stone massage" as "hospital massage" in a booking demo.

    What I changed

    I tested a second speech engine with keyword boosting, then went back to the first one.

    Still watching
  • What went wrong

    Sunny said the same answer twice in one turn. A silent note-taking step made the AI answer again.

    What I changed

    I changed how that step runs, so she answers once.

    Fixed

Tools and texting

  • What went wrong

    Sunny said she would text a link, but the text did not always arrive.

    What I changed

    The phone platform was missing some functions. It took five fixes to make texts send. I confirmed it with a test.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    Two texts in one turn: the second one, a note to the owner, was empty and failed without any warning.

    What I changed

    I split them across two turns. Both texts now send.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    The main agent sometimes answered by itself instead of passing the question to the right specialist.

    What I changed

    I fixed the routing so each question goes to the specialist.

    Fixed
  • What went wrong

    Values that should fill in automatically reached a tool as empty text.

    What I changed

    I put the values directly in the instructions and made them required.

    Fixed

What testing taught me

  • Test the whole path. A text that "should send" means nothing until it arrives.
  • Do not let the AI fill gaps. No made-up numbers, prices or hours.
  • Speech is not text. Numbers and names get misheard, so confirm them.

Question askedMy daughter is 3 years old. Should I worry about autism?

Question askedMy son has autism. Where should I go?

Question askedMy son is 7 years old. He has ADHD. Is there any support in school?

What Sunny didShe explained the Individual Education Plan and wrote a short email the parent can send to the school. Then she explained two funding programs, SSAH and ACSD, and said neither is automatic for ADHD.

Worked

This is the answer I corrected earlier. She now says these programs depend on how much ADHD affects daily life, and she does not deny the family.

From receptionist to navigator

My first idea was an AI receptionist for autism clinics. As I learned more, I saw two problems. Clinics already have booking tools and do not want to switch. And parents need more than someone to answer the phone. They need guidance while they wait.

So I changed the product. Sunny now works next to the tools a clinic already uses, and focuses on the family during the wait. I also widened who it helps: therapy practices, schools and family support organizations, not only autism clinics.

I keep learning from real parents by reading their questions in support groups and noting the words they use.

Where it stands

  • Live now

    Phone line, website chat, text links, booking, and English and French support.

  • Next

    First clinic pilots, so I can test Sunny with real families and real calls.

  • What I learned

    Trust matters more than speed. In this space, a confident wrong answer does more harm than no answer.
